# Peer Review — MER Theory v0.3.0

**Reviewer perspective**: Theoretical physics referee reviewing v0.3.0 artifact extracted from `theory/0.3.0/mer-theory.pdf`.

## Overall
The most important improvement in v0.3.0 is that the theory is now expressed in tensor form. That makes the paper substantially more coherent, because the core objects are no longer opaque ad-hoc recipes. Adding Noether conservation laws and a dimensional table is also meaningful progress. The discussion remains stronger at the conceptual level than at the level of physical justification, especially for the gravity applications, but the manuscript now shows enough mathematical structure to be treated seriously.

## Strengths
- The covariant action principle turns a bundle of postulated equations into a system that can actually be reviewed.
- The explicit field equations, especially the tension between exactness and specification of boundary conditions, give reviewers concrete things to evaluate.
- The Noether charge calculation is a real addition: it connects the model to standard conservation-law reasoning.
- The six falsifiable predictions with quantitative estimates begin what this manuscript needs most: an experimentally testable core.

## Main objections
- The gravity claims still outrun the demonstrations. Convergence of GR in the `ϵ_μ → 0` limit is asserted but not shown at the level of a full derivation.
- The numerical simulation section reads like a blend of actual algorithm description and messaging about future capability. Reviewers will try to read it too literally, so it needs clearer boundary-setting language.
- Some quantitative claims that sound experimental—parameter estimates, posterior bounds for MCMC—are not derived from real data and should be labeled as mock or illustrative.

## Readiness
v0.3.0 is closer to a valid preprint. To submit under current standards it still needs missing derivations, tighter precision on experimental predictions, and disciplined labeling of what is done versus what is planned.
