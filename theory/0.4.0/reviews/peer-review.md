## Peer Review — MER Theory v0.4.0

**Reviewer perspective**: Theoretical physics referee reviewing v0.4.0 artifact: `theory/0.4.0/mer-theory.txt`.

### Overall
This manuscript has made a major organizational and conceptual leap. The epistemic tiering—Postulate / Theorem / Conjecture—is the right structure for a physics monograph with genuinely speculative elements, and the expanded chapter layout gives the work the appearance of a serious research monograph. The peer-reviewer-friendly organization materially improves the impression left on a first reading. The gravitational and entanglement claims are still not developed to the standard of a full derivation, but the presentational framework is now strong enough that the manuscript can be evaluated piece by piece rather than rejected as a whole.

### Strengths
- The Postulate / Theorem / Conjecture stratification is the strongest addition. It makes the theory easier to evaluate and to revise iteratively.
- The metric variation treatment is significantly more complete and aligns with mainstream variational-GR presentation.
- The Noether charge calculation and exact conservation law now have enough detail that a specialist can verify them.
- The Bullet Cluster and ER=EPR sections are now framed as physical predictions rather than speculative draping, which is a real improvement.

### Main objections
- The interaction stress tensor and scale-vector field equation deserve their own detailed derivation subsections. They are stated cleanly but still read like summarized assertions.
- The Madelung recovery should include explicit second-order scale-mismatch perturbation terms and state the approximation regime with sharper bounds.
- The experimental predictions table should be restored from v0.3.0-style quantitative form, because the current prose loses precision that specialist reviewers need.
- The MCMC and parameter-estimation claims still need an ESS/Gelman-Rubin section before they can be treated as empirical.

### Readiness
v0.4.0 is now close to a valid monograph draft. It needs tighter derivations for the interaction tensor, stronger experimental-prediction formatting, and rigorous labeling of which computations are exact versus illustrative before submission.
