Abstract
Modern theoretical physics is fundamentally bifurcated: Quantum Mechanics (QM) formulates reality in terms of discrete, probabilistic state vectors residing in a Hilbert space H, whereas General Relativity (GR) models spacetime as a continuous, deterministic 4D pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M, g_{\mu\nu}). Multi-scale Emergent Reality (MER) Theory resolves this fundamental incompatibility by positing that physical laws, observed symmetries, and probability distributions are scale-dependent projections of a single, continuous 4D covariant field action S_MER. In this comprehensive monograph draft, we establish the axiomatic foundation of MER Theory, strictly categorizing all physical assertions into Axiomatic Postulates, Proven Mathematical Theorems, and Empirical Conjectures. We present the complete covariant action principle, perform step-by-step metric variations to derive the full stress-energy-momentum tensor T_{\mu\nu}^{(MER)}, prove Noether conservation laws for internal phase invariance and spacetime translations, and demonstrate exact limiting asymptotic regimes that recover standard General Relativity (epsilon^mu -> 0) and Non-Relativistic Quantum Mechanics (epsilon^0 << 1 via Madelung hydrodynamics). Furthermore, we provide a non-linear field-stress mechanism for cosmic cluster collisions (resolving Bullet Cluster lensing anomalies without undiscovered dark matter particles) and map EPR quantum entanglement directly to ER topological separatrix throats.

Chapter 1: Epistemic Framework and Foundational Postulates

1.1 The Epistemic Scale Gap
Traditional quantum field theory (QFT) and general relativity implicitly assume scale neutrality: the hypothesis that the fundamental equations of motion hold independently of the observer's relative observation resolution. MER Theory asserts that probability P(x, t) is not an intrinsic ontological property of physical reality, but an epistemic artifact arising when an observer operating at a macroscopic scale L_O measures a microscopic system operating at scale L_S, where L_O >> L_S.
